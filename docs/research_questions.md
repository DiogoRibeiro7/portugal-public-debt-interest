# Analysis plan

## Descriptive sequence

1. Plot interest expenditure in euros and as a percentage of GDP.
2. Locate maxima, minima, and turning points.
3. Compare the interest ratio with the debt ratio and implicit interest rate.
4. Decompose the difference between the primary and overall balance.
5. Compare market yields with the effective cost of the outstanding portfolio.

## Historical regimes

- 1995–1998: convergence towards monetary union.
- 1999–2007: early euro period.
- 2008–2010: global financial crisis.
- 2011–2014: sovereign-debt crisis and adjustment programme.
- 2015–2019: ECB intervention and refinancing.
- 2020–2021: pandemic.
- 2022 onward: inflation and monetary tightening.

Regime labels are descriptive hypotheses. Statistical break detection should be used to test, not merely restate, the periodisation.

## Counterfactuals

- Reprice the Portuguese debt ratio at another country's implicit interest rate.
- Hold the debt ratio at its pandemic peak.
- Apply 50, 100, and 200 basis-point rate shocks.
- Compare immediate full-pass-through arithmetic with gradual refinancing.
- Estimate the primary balance required for debt stabilisation under alternative nominal-growth and effective-rate assumptions.

## European comparison

Use Portugal, Spain, Italy, Greece, Ireland, the euro area, Germany, and the Netherlands. Every panel observation must use the same Eurostat transaction, sector, and unit.

## Opportunity-cost comparisons

Compare interest expenditure with public investment, education, healthcare, defence, and selected tax revenues. These are scale comparisons, not claims that the entire interest bill could have been reallocated without changing financing conditions.
